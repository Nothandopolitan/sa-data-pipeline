3. Limits of these results

  • Correlation only: nothing here shows that unemployment causes crime.
  • Small sample: 9 provinces over 10 years, and consecutive years are not independent, so p-values are optimistic. 36 tests were run, so a few "significant" results are expected by chance.
  • Shared national trends and the COVID-19 year (2020/21) can create correlations between series that are not related.
  • Rates use the working-age population (15 to 64) as the denominator, not total population, so they differ from published SAPS rates.
  • Crime is per financial year (April to March); unemployment is the average of the four quarters in the same year. Only the official unemployment rate is used.
  • SAPS counts are reported crime, which depends on reporting and policing practice.
